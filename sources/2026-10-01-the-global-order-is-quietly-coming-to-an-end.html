The global order is quietly coming to an end

Oct 1, 2026 · 19 min · 37 sources

"What happens when the architect of the world order decides to tear it down?"

For eight decades, the United States built and upheld much of the international system that shaped the postwar world.

It led military alliances. It backed global institutions. It protected major trade routes. It presented itself, however imperfectly, as a defender of sovereignty, democracy, and international rules.

Then, in December 2025, the White House released a new National Security Strategy.

At first glance, it was another government policy document. A plan for dealing with threats. A statement of priorities. A roadmap for American power.

Read more closely, though, and it looks like something else.

Less like a foreign-policy roadmap than a farewell statement to the post-Cold War international order.

The document signals a visible retreat from the institutional and hegemonic architecture that the United States built, financed, and led for nearly eight decades.

That is a remarkable thing for an American strategy document to do.

Because the United States is still one of the most powerful countries in the world. It still has the strongest military. The dollar remains the dominant international currency. Its alliances still stretch across Europe and Asia.

This is not a story about America suddenly disappearing.

It is about something more important.

It is about what happens when the country with the greatest ability to shape the international system no longer wants to pay the price of doing so.
