The War That Broke the Soviet Union

Nov 18, 2026 · 16 min · 24 sources

Kabul. December 27th, 1979.

It's December 27th, 1979. We're at the Tajbeg Palace in Kabul, and it's lunchtime. Afghanistan's president, Hafizullah Amin, is sitting down to eat with his ministers. Soviet soldiers are already stationed in the city. Officially, they're there to help him.

What Amin doesn't know is that one of the palace cooks is working for the KGB. The food has been poisoned.

The president and his ministers start to collapse. Amin slips into unconsciousness. Two Soviet doctors at the palace, who know nothing about the plot, work desperately to bring him back. Somehow, he survives.

A few hours later, Amin is lying in bed when the shooting starts.

Hundreds of Soviet troops have surrounded the palace. Among them are men from a unit called the Muslim Battalion, along with KGB special forces. The KGB teams storm the building. The army holds a ring around it.

Amin doesn't survive the assault. He most likely dies of gunshot wounds. Many of his male relatives were killed or executed. His wife, his daughter and his grandchildren are later imprisoned.

The very next day, Moscow installs a new president, a rival communist named Babrak Karmal.

The Soviet Union has just killed the man it was supposed to be propping up. This is only the first day of a war that will last almost ten years.
