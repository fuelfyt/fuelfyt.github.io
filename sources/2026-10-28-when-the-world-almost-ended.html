46 years ago, the world almost ended

Oct 28, 2026 · 14 min · 19 sources

The Pentagon. June 3rd, 1980.

It's about two-thirty in the morning. Deep beneath the Pentagon, at the National Military Command Center, computers start flashing a warning. It also appears at the North American Air Defense Command, buried inside Cheyenne Mountain, and at Site R, the Pentagon's backup command post inside Raven Rock Mountain, Pennsylvania.

The message is simple. The Soviet Union has just launched a nuclear attack on the United States.

None of those missiles existed, however. Between October 1979 and June 1980, America's warning systems sent out one false alarm after another. On four occasions, warning screens showed Soviet missiles on their way to North America.
